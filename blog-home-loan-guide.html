A first-time applicant's guide to home loans in India

Published 12 June 2026 · 6 min read

Most first-time home loan applicants assume the biggest hurdle is the interest rate. In practice, the bigger risk to your timeline is usually documentation — specifically, not knowing in advance what a lender will actually ask for. Here's what typically matters most.

Income proof lenders trust most

For salaried applicants, lenders generally want the latest three months of salary slips and six months of bank statements showing that salary being credited. For self-employed applicants, two to three years of ITR filings and the corresponding computation of income matter more than a single strong year — lenders are looking for a stable trend, not a one-off spike.

Property documents come before income documents

A common delay: applicants assemble their income file perfectly but haven't confirmed the property has a clear, marketable title. Before applying, it helps to have the chain of ownership documents, the latest property tax receipt, and — for under-construction property — the builder's approvals in hand. A property with title issues can stall a file no matter how strong your income profile is.

Your existing obligations matter more than your salary alone

Lenders typically assess what portion of your income is already committed to other EMIs before deciding how much more you can service. This is often expressed as FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio). Paying down a small existing loan, or closing a credit card that's carrying a running balance, before you apply can meaningfully change your eligible loan amount.

The three most common causes of delay

  • Mismatch between the name on PAN, Aadhaar and bank account records
  • Address proof that doesn't match the current residence
  • Missing co-applicant documents when a co-applicant's income is being considered for eligibility

A practical starting checklist

Before your first conversation with a lender or advisor, it's worth having ready: PAN and Aadhaar, last three months' salary slips or two years of ITR, six months of bank statements, and the property's basic ownership documents. Having these organised up front is usually the single biggest factor in how quickly a file moves.
